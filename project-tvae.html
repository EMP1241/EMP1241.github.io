Thermal Vent Atlas Explorer

I independently built TVAE for Sylvan Lab to explore hydrothermal vents, where heated, mineral-rich water emerges from the seafloor. I connected vent records with ocean-depth maps and tectonic boundaries, developed comparisons and researcher-added fields, and built imports, documented exports, and saved workspaces. I delivered version 1.3.0 for lab review and internal use, with local overview maps for offline work.

A geographic workspace for the deep ocean

A vent catalogue lists locations and attributes, but a researcher also needs the surrounding ocean basin, ridge system, and nearby sites to interpret them geographically. I built the atlas that brings those records into the same view as seafloor relief and tectonic boundaries, then added selection, comparison, and research-note workflows. I integrated local overview maps so the broad geographic context remains available offline.

My role

I independently designed and developed TVAE from Sylvan Lab requirements, built its online and offline mapping workflows, and delivered it to the lab for review and internal use.

I built tools to bring in data, filter and compare sites, measure distances, summarize records, and save an investigation. Exports include the sources behind the selected records so the recipient can see where the information came from.

Seafloor context at the right scale

The source layer preserves 721 vent-field records from InterRidge v3.4. The local CSV also contains twenty added vent/subsite records and one test/example row; these additions are not InterRidge observations. GEBCO 2025 supplies seafloor relief and PB2002 supplies tectonic plate boundaries. The gallery shows a public source vent in its wider ocean setting.

From vents to geomicrobiology

Geomicrobiology studies how microbes interact with geology and chemistry. I built separate fields and export choices for the original vent catalogue, researchers’ biological notes, and imported data. Researchers can inspect them together and still see where each item came from. I kept genetic potential distinct from observed activity: genes can suggest what a microbe could do, but measurements are needed to show what it was doing.

Feature add-ons for research questions

I developed nearby-site searches and side-by-side comparisons for two to twelve selected vents so a researcher can inspect the same fields across a chosen group. I added activity and depth summaries, exploratory plots, annotations, and a cruise waypoint planner for organizing candidate locations. I also built an optional Python helper that samples seafloor-depth grids from NetCDF or GMT files and retains the input-source context with its output.

I built CSV and point GeoJSON imports that preserve custom fields and keep their review separate from the base catalogue. I also implemented export controls for selected rows, filtered rows, or the complete view, with separate field choices for source records, research additions, and imports. This makes the contents of a handoff explicit.

I delivered version 1.3.0 to Sylvan Lab for review and internal use. I also compared the Windows and macOS downloads with the intended application, data, helper tools, and startup scripts; the files matched. That comparison checked package contents. The catalogue keeps original vent records distinguishable from local additions and the example row.

A workspace that can travel

I integrated local GEBCO overview tiles and hillshade so a saved investigation retains broad seafloor context without an internet connection. The local tiles are native through zoom level 5; closer views enlarge the available tiles rather than adding finer source data. A saved session remembers filters, selected vents, imported rows, notes, route points, and map view, but it does not duplicate the full atlas. When connected, Esri ocean layers support closer inspection. An optional local Python service can write atlas edits to the base CSV and save a backup. Browser imports stay separate unless a user explicitly exports them or includes them in a session.
