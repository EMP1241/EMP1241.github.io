Why I built it

A map is only useful to a research team if the evidence behind it remains accessible. I built the workbench to keep original files, parsed records, spatial features, review decisions, and investigation scope together.

My role

I independently designed and developed the application: its evidence model, spatial database integration, intake and validation workflows, browser interface, saved investigations, and export behavior. I maintain the system as the research requirements evolve.

Original evidence stays inspectable

Intake preserves original files and identifies versions before producing parsed records and map companions. Technical checks, operator intake decisions, and review notes remain separate records alongside the material being investigated.

A spatial investigation with memory

PostGIS connects evidence records with geometry. The Leaflet interface supports mapped inspection and linked provenance, while saved investigations retain source and boundary versions, filters, and method context. A reviewer can follow a displayed evidence feature back to the evidence and geographic support used.

Engineering the handoff

Python/FastAPI manages bounded parsing, validation, transactions, and exports. PostgreSQL/PostGIS holds the evidence and geometry; TypeScript connects the browser workflow. Exports carry source identities, selected scope, methods, and review context so a handoff contains more than a final map.

Where it fits

The workbench is an independent application. Research Architect generated the project’s coordination system; the NWSW research case explains the actual investigation, my GIS contributions, and cross-team synthesis. The gallery here shows the working software using synthetic QA examples.
