About

From tree rings to other worlds.

I study geographic information science and technology at Texas A&M University. I am also a U.S. Marine Corps veteran and geospatial software developer.

I independently created Tree-Ring Atlas Explorer and developed its import checks, climate-data preparation, linked charts, and documented exports. Demonstrating it led to my paid research appointment. I also built Thermal Vent Atlas Explorer for Sylvan Lab, integrating vent records with seafloor relief, plate boundaries, and separately identified biological context.

I lead a Texas A&M student research team conducting New World Screwworm analysis for the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA). I recover missing information, reconcile sources, prepare geographic data, and test location uncertainty. I created and lead COLE, corrected and integrated its feature additions, and released the application for web and Windows. For Mars Explorer, I lead a five-person team and developed the coordinate handling, spatial database, and terrain-processing workflow connecting selected landscapes with NASA mission records.

Research direction

The next frontier starts with better tools.

I’m interested in how maps and research data can help people understand a site and plan work on its surface, including on other planets. My current projects develop the data preparation, field methods, and software needed for that direction.

Across these projects, I focus on keeping original evidence accessible, recording how an analysis was produced, and giving researchers tools they can use in the lab and in the field.

I also study ancient Martian hydrology through a comparative review of published work on rainfall, snowmelt, groundwater, and subglacial drainage. This literature-based study examines how different processes could form ancient valley networks and is separate from my Mars Explorer software.
