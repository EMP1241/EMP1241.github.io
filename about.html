About

From tree rings to other worlds.

I study geographic information science and technology at Texas A&M University. I am also a U.S. Marine Corps veteran and geospatial software developer.

My work ranges from preparing tree-growth measurements and climate records, through mapping hydrothermal vents against seafloor relief and tectonic plate boundaries, to reconstructing bounded Martian terrain in 3D from orbital elevation and imagery. The vent atlas keeps microbial context tied to its source. Across these projects, I build tools that connect original sources, spatial data, and reviewable analysis.

I am a paid research assistant in Torbenson Lab. I lead the New World Screwworm geographic information systems (GIS) research project and COLE (Cost Of Living Explorer), a county-level decision-support application, and I lead the team developing Mars Explorer. My work combines research design, data preparation, application architecture, field methods, and feature development that makes a tool useful beyond its first demonstration.

Research direction

The next frontier starts with better tools.

I’m interested in spatial decision support for site characterization and surface operations, including planetary environments. My current work builds the data handling, field methods, and research software behind that direction.

Across these projects, I focus on keeping original evidence accessible, recording how an analysis was produced, and giving researchers tools they can use in the lab and in the field.
