Making complex collections usable

Tree-ring measurements carry decades or centuries of observations, but files, measurement types, site metadata, and climate records rarely arrive in one consistent form. TRAE brings them into a workspace for locating, screening, comparing, and organizing records while keeping their origins visible.

My role and research impact

I independently created the early prototype in response to Torbenson Lab’s research needs, demonstrated it, and earned a paid research assistant appointment to continue development. I maintain the application and its companion tools.

I used TRAE to derive, clean, organize, and export study data for a co-authored manuscript in preparation. That work connects software development directly to an active research workflow.

Maps and graphs that work together

Selecting a site links its geographic context with measurement plots and summaries. Raw total, earlywood, and latewood widths retain their measurement identity; imported CRN chronologies remain separate. Chart smoothing supports visual screening while source measurements and exports remain intact.

Researchers can compare records, inspect series coverage, and explore climate associations. Raw-width site means, site-mean adjusted-latewood residuals, EPS estimates, and Pearson correlations support exploratory screening. Chronology development, calibration, and scientific validation remain downstream research steps.

The feature add-ons behind the workflow

Climate Builder converts monthly records into annual or seasonal targets with explicit month windows, aggregation, year assignment, and completeness settings. PRISM support samples climate rasters at site locations and produces provenance-linked tables.

The NetCDF companion inspects variables and previews eligible annual series. ROXAS Prep organizes existing anatomical-analysis outputs into usable bundles. The WalDendro YEAR cleaner reviews annual blocks, sorts records, handles duplicate choices, and writes cleaned copies with reports. These tools address the preparation work that often determines whether scientific data is usable.

Optional local AI extracts quoted clues from the current import-review text. TRAE checks that those clues appear in the supplied row and field; the researcher decides whether metadata changes. Measurement values remain untouched.

Reviewed imports and reusable work

Temporary imports support quick inspection; reviewed managed imports add reusable library records. Parser warnings, measurement scales, identity conflicts, and metadata gaps are exposed for review. Original files, hashes, and import manifests preserve the path back to the source.

From inspection to analysis

Shareable .TRAE session files save the working view, temporary ring-file imports, climate choices, checked sites, and notes. The managed research library remains separate. CSV tables, GeoJSON, screening reports, and analysis bundles with R, Python, and MATLAB loader scripts support downstream work; export metadata records the selected data, units, and method settings.
