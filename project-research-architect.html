The brand and my role

PhalAInx AI is my independent software brand. I created it to develop tools for structured research and coordinated human-and-AI work. I own the product direction, application design, workflow requirements, and integration, with AI assistance in implementation, research, and review.

Research Architect is one software product under that brand. A separate multi-LLM collaboration application is in progress. The two share an interest in clear context, accountable assignments, and useful handoffs, while serving distinct product roles.

Research Architect

Research Architect turns a project brief into a structured research workflow. It defines the question, scope, source expectations, roles, bounded jobs, dependencies, and review points before the work is combined. Each contributor receives project-specific context and instructions for producing findings, recording uncertainty, and handing work back for review.

The framework keeps sources, methods, findings, decisions, and unresolved gaps connected across workstreams. Persistent project records and role-specific activation material let work resume with its original context. Human review determines when a plan, claim, or handoff is ready to move forward.

My design work focuses on how a research team moves between individual assignments and shared synthesis. A generated role or setup kit represents an assignment; the resulting work still needs evidence, method records, and review before it becomes part of the project’s findings.

The multi-LLM collaboration workspace

This separate application is intended to let multiple LLM systems—including Claude, GPT, Gemini, and other supported providers—collaborate through the same interface. The user would keep project context, assignments, work status, evidence, and review in one workspace rather than manually coordinating every exchange across separate applications.

The working desktop prototype already provides local project creation, role and job editing, task workspaces, decision and gap records, source and claim records, and versioned agent-kit planning. The gallery shows its real project and kit-review interfaces using QA examples.

How the prototype is built

Electron hosts a React/TypeScript interface with local SQLite project records. Original imported files can be copied and identified by SHA-256, while notes, outputs, review statements, and plan revisions retain their own histories. A deterministic kit compiler prepares role-specific setup material from a reviewed plan.

Kit preview and export bind to the approved plan revision so that changing the plan requires another review. The desktop workflow distinguishes drafts, approvals, prepared setup material, and completed work. Those distinctions make it easier to understand what the software has actually saved or produced.

Development status

The multi-provider collaboration workflow remains in development. Current desktop planning and review features provide a foundation; provider connection and execution support are being developed separately. The dated screenshots demonstrate the local application features shown, rather than live Claude, GPT, or Gemini collaboration.

The next product goal is to connect supported runtimes and coordinate their work inside the shared interface while keeping provider sign-in, tool access, and consequential project decisions under the user’s control.
