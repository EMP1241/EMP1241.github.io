C03 / Geospatial decision support

COLE · Cost Of Living Explorer

National household-cost, climate, and county data in a local decision-support workflow that makes its assumptions visible.

Read the case study ↓
1 / 3

The problem

Households need a way to narrow county options before becoming attached to a destination, while seeing how estimated costs, take-home income, climate preferences, and personal assumptions affect a shortlist.

My role & collaborators

I lead COLE’s application design, integration, documentation, and presentation. I directed requirements and development, with AI assistance in implementation, data preparation, and verification.

Bingqian contributed the natural-hazard add-on, including its original hazard logic, FEMA preparation, presentation, and tests. It was integrated under my direction, with that attribution preserved.

Data & method

A local Python/Flask/Waitress application and Leaflet interface combine EPI household estimates, Census county geography and place/ZCTA relationships, NOAA county temperature estimates, and an optional FEMA-derived hazard feature. Source identities, geography vintages, and data fingerprints are retained.

Implemented workflow

The app moves from purpose and method to criteria, county map, and results. Users can inspect costs, compare three counties, set optional cost, temperature, and reserve criteria, and try explicitly acknowledged personal cost replacements. Draft criteria remain separate from accepted results.

Result & limits

A functioning local application with national county coverage, source-linked detail, and an offline delivery workflow. Current source is version 0.6.0; earlier portable releases predate some source changes.

County estimates are generalized renter household costs, not predictions of individual expenses. Income is after tax; NOAA values are long-term averages. Hazard ranks are not disaster probabilities or personal safety guarantees.

A household-driven search

Users choose a supported household profile, enter annual or monthly take-home pay, and add expenses the published budget does not cover. Optional limits narrow the search by state, individual cost categories, long-term temperatures, and natural-hazard measures. A live count previews the draft; submitting applies it to the map, county list, details, and comparisons.

Making cost assumptions visible

COLE removes EPI’s modeled taxes because the user supplies after-tax income. Individual cost categories can be replaced with known monthly expenses while the original EPI figures remain visible beside them. Replacements apply across every searched county, with an explicit acknowledgment of that limitation. An optional reserve percentage checks whether estimated costs leave the requested share of income; an income range uses its lower endpoint.

Discovery, lookup, and comparison

Discovery returns counties meeting every active criterion. A city or ZIP-area lookup retains its associated counties and identifies criteria they miss, including places crossing county lines. Users can inspect a county without adding it to comparison, then explicitly compare up to three. Editing draft criteria preserves accepted work until the next submission.

Local delivery

Windows preview packages include a runtime and prepared local data for interactive exploration. The current source has separate verification records from earlier packages. The implemented workflow centers on searching, inspecting assumptions, and comparing counties; export and saved-session features remain future work.
