Deployment scope

What the package makes available

  • 41 method routes/domain cases, eight shared foundations, and 32 specialist guides.
  • Three new complete synthetic research cases: clustered workflow comparison; prediction with nested group selection and reserved evaluation data; and target-population standardization with uncertainty and unsupported-population checks.
  • Each case includes a declared plan, fixed data, explicit offline Python analysis, calculated results, diagnostics, invalid alternatives, and bounded conclusions. Three accompanying guides explain the full research sequence, analysis-selection safeguards, and practical generalization.
  • 134 source records, 38 local reference texts, and 270 indexed records. Five original papers were added as source records; four had targeted original-paper review and one remained metadata-only after failed full-text access. No new full texts were bundled.
  • Seven retained operational cases remain separate from the three synthetic teaching cases. Their earlier evidence and interpretation boundaries are unchanged.

These counts describe available materials. They do not establish comprehensive disciplinary coverage, complete review of every source, or measured improvements in scientific outcomes. Metadata-only records are distinct from local full-text extracts.

How guidance reaches a participant

The compiler includes the selected methods and their required specialist/source dependencies. Long chapters and papers stay outside routine startup. Local catalog, search, and exact reading use bounded UTF-8 responses with source-hash-bound continuation, so the selected material can be reconstructed completely without silently truncating it. Retrieval makes no network or model calls.

What the teaching cases establish

The new results are reproducible calculations on invented data. They illustrate how duplicates, clustered observations, model selection, and population coverage affect an analysis. The program supports fixed teaching designs; it is not a general replacement for statistical software. Its data-flow separation does not add an access-control feature, and the framework does not automatically enforce sound research conduct or prove that an agent selects the right method.

The retained casebook records these existing successes. It does not create seven new experiments, and none of those underlying operations was rerun for this knowledge release.

What these checks do not establish

Unchanged operational repair suites were not replayed. No live approvals, closed work orders, or Hub/Library state were changed. Local process checks do not measure fresh-model research quality, total billing savings, or autonomous scientific validity. Earlier successful operations remain evidence for their recorded scenarios.

Limitation: adding someone after the project starts

A new person and their new agent both need to catch up. The person must learn the project; the agent needs its decisions, evidence, terminology, dependencies, and unresolved work. Even an experienced team member introducing a fresh agent faces that agent’s context gap. A generated kit and Library access do not automatically transfer the established team’s working understanding.

The project keeps moving during onboarding. If new decisions and corrections accumulate faster than the newcomer can absorb them, the catch-up gap can persist. The lead and existing team must explain prior work, resolve misunderstandings, and check the new pair’s output; existing agents may also spend time and inference resources helping. That effort can slow the team and make late expansion impractical within its time and cost budget.

This is a limitation of the underlying AI technology and research workflow. Adding a kit does not guarantee more useful capacity, and reliable automatic late-stage onboarding has not been demonstrated. Feasibility depends on the role, project pace, and retained context. Restoring an established workstream is a different task. The documentation update describes this limitation; it does not implement a fix.
