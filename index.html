Featured case study

NWSW Spatial Evidence Workbench

The problem

Research teams need to connect mapped records and conclusions to the exact sources, revisions, and review decisions behind them. Integrating documents, tables, GIS files, and contributor packages requires preserving that context through intake, review, and analysis.

My role

As project lead and application owner, I coordinate research contributions and own the development, integration, operation, and maintenance of the workbench. The research is team work; original contributors and sources retain attribution.

Data and method

The application preserves original files and source identities, validates incoming contributor packages, stores spatial records in PostgreSQL/PostGIS, and records explicit review states. Saved investigations and exports retain their source versions, filter scope, methods, and review history.

The interface uses TypeScript and Leaflet, supported by a Python/FastAPI backend. Bundled terrain supports offline map access.

What I found

The implemented workflow keeps file acceptance, automated checks, and human research review separate. Original sources and review history remain traceable through mapped investigations and reproducible exports.

The application is implemented. The broader team assessment remains ongoing; this portfolio does not present a completed intelligence estimate or a released prediction model.

Tree-Ring Atlas Explorer

The problem

Tree-ring measurements, site metadata, and climate records were scattered across collections, making consistent discovery and comparison difficult.

What I did

I created an offline-first research application for locating, screening, comparing, and organizing tree-ring records with climate context. I implemented reviewed imports, site-to-climate matching, provenance tracking, and reproducible exports for downstream analysis.

What I found

The prototype led to a paid research assistant appointment in Torbenson Lab. I used TRAE to derive, clean, organize, and export data for a co-authored manuscript in preparation. Climate comparisons are exploratory screening, not validated climate reconstructions.

Field research

Smartphone GNSS Error

The problem

How does pedestrian crowding relate to actual smartphone positioning error, and does device-reported uncertainty reflect that error?

What I did

I collected 781 retained readings from three smartphones across five crowd categories using ArcGIS Field Maps and a published NGS reference. I analyzed actual versus reported horizontal error in Python using Welch's ANOVA and adjusted regression models.

What I found

Within this study, at 41+ people the iPhone 13 averaged 109.4 ft of actual error versus 16.7 ft of reported uncertainty. Reported uncertainty understated observed positional error.

This was a single-site observational study. Repeated readings and reference uncertainty limit inference; the study does not isolate the contribution of any individual receiver frequency or test E911-delivered locations.
