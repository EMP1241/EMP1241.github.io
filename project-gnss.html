Smartphone GNSS Error

An observational study of actual positioning error and device-reported uncertainty across pedestrian crowd categories.

Read the case study ↓
1 / 3
Project context

Field researcher

Three smartphones

Five crowd categories

The problem

How does pedestrian crowding relate to actual smartphone positioning error, and does device-reported uncertainty reflect that error?

My role, data & method

I collected 781 retained readings from three smartphones across five crowd categories using ArcGIS Field Maps and a published NGS reference. I analyzed actual versus reported horizontal error in Python using Welch's ANOVA and adjusted regression models.

What I found

Within this study, at 41+ people the iPhone 13 averaged 109.4 ft of actual error versus 16.7 ft of reported uncertainty. Reported uncertainty understated observed positional error.

This was a single-site observational study. Repeated readings and reference uncertainty limit inference; the study does not isolate the contribution of any individual receiver frequency or test E911-delivered locations.

Illustrative scenario

What a 50-acre footprint looks like

This hypothetical present-day New Orleans example uses 30 synthetic reported positions and overlapping 171-foot modeled buffers to show an approximately 50-acre combined footprint. It illustrates how a precise-looking point on a screen can correspond to a much larger area on the ground.

The green points show reported positions; the red shaded union shows the modeled area. This is a visual scenario, not a reconstruction of Hurricane Katrina or a record of actual emergency calls.

Katrina informs the scenario’s setting: official reports describe damaged communications, disrupted dispatch systems, and rescue-coordination problems. Read the White House account and search-and-rescue lessons.
